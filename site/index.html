Why

Speaking shouldn't take this much effort.

As ALS progresses, speech and hand movement both get harder. Typing every sentence is slow and tiring, and many communication apps are dense, small and easy to mis-tap.

Speed

Two taps to speak

Choose the place you're in, then tap a phrase written for that situation. No typing for everyday conversation.

Effort

Big, calm, forgiving

Large targets, one category at a time, no sideways scrolling, and protection against tremor and accidental taps.

Reliability

Always works

Fully offline, plays even when the phone is on silent, and falls back safely if its data can't load — it is someone's voice.

How it works

Where are you? → What do you want to say?

1

Pick a place

Soft, high-contrast cards for the places you go. Add your own, like Church or Work.

2

Tap a phrase

Natural, first-person phrases grouped into big tabs. Yes / No are always one tap away.

3

It's spoken and shown

Said aloud right away, with a haptic tap, and displayed full-screen so the listener can read it too.

Features

Everything within reach, nothing in the way.

Quick replies on every screen

✓ Yes and ✕ No are pinned to the bottom of every screen. More opens the rest — Maybe, Thank you, Wait a moment — with no scrolling.

SOS that can't go off by accident

Hold for one second to sound a loud, repeated emergency message with a red alert screen. VoiceOver and Switch Control users activate it directly.

Type when nothing fits

Type-to-speak with suggestions and recent messages. One tap saves what you typed as a new phrase in the place you're in.

Made personal

Favorites, phrases that follow you everywhere, and details like {name} filled in when spoken. Caregivers can add, edit and reorder anything.

Simple editing for caregivers

An Edit mode right on each board: tap a phrase to change it, add phrases to any category, and organize categories and places.

Your voice, your way

Choose any iOS voice — including your own recorded Personal Voice — and set speed, pitch and volume with big − / + buttons.

Accessibility first

Designed for limited speech and limited movement.

Accessibility isn't a settings page here — it shaped every screen, and it's tested automatically.

  • Tap, hold or dwell. Hold-to-select ignores brushes and tremor; dwell works with head, eye and AssistiveTouch pointers.
  • Tremor filtering. A second tap within 0.4 s is ignored, so a shaky double-tap doesn't speak twice.
  • VoiceOver, Switch Control, Voice Control. Every control is labelled; phrase grids scan as groups.
  • Dynamic Type. Text scales to the largest sizes; the always-visible bar offers the Large Content Viewer instead.
  • High contrast. A black-and-yellow theme, also switched on by iOS Increase Contrast.
  • 60 pt+ targets. Big buttons with clear pressed states and haptic confirmation.

Under the hood

Native, offline, and tested.

Stack

Swift and SwiftUI for iOS 17+, SwiftData for storage, AVSpeechSynthesizer for on-device speech. No accounts, no backend, no network.

Architecture

MVVM. A single SpeechCoordinator handles every spoken phrase; SpeechService wraps the synthesizer behind a protocol for interrupt, queue, stop and emergency.

Testing

Swift Testing for models, seeding, speech and view models; XCUITest flows with performAccessibilityAudit(); exact WCAG contrast checks for every color pair.
